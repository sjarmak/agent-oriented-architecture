# Thirty-five years of scheduling under an open sky: a literature review

The operations-research literature behind *The factory's empty seams are solved scheduling problems* (`docs/or-for-software-factories.md`), reviewed as a body of work.

**Status:** #research · **Date:** 2026-07-10
**Method:** six parallel corpus sweeps over SciX/ADS (~32M documents, full arXiv mirror, 46% full-text coverage), one per facet: exact MIP scheduling, network optimization, stochastic and dynamic programming, nonlinear programming and duality, modeling languages and solver practice, metaheuristics and search-based software engineering. Roughly 195 retrieval and full-text tool calls; every claim below is anchored to an ADS bibcode. Raw sweep output: `scix_experiments/docs/or_optimization_survey_2026-07.md`.
**Reading caveat:** ADS citation counts are astronomy-weighted. Chen et al.'s almost-linear max-flow result, a STOC best paper, shows 45 citations here while the Louvain paper shows 3,588; rank the theory by venue and result, not by this corpus's counts.

---

A literature review usually maps a field onto itself. This one maps two fields onto a third: what observatory operations and mathematical programming have published, read as design evidence for automated software factories, a domain in which the corpus contains exactly zero direct prior art. That absence is the review's first finding and its standing invitation. Nothing here applies MIP to merge queues, restless bandits to CI, or min-cut to module boundaries; the nearest neighbors are datacenter RL schedulers and grid job placement. Everything else in this review is transfer material, and the review's job is to establish how load-bearing each source is before anyone builds on it.

## 1. Exact scheduling grew up on telescopes

The deepest applied lineage in the corpus is observatory scheduling, and its historical arc is itself instructive. Hubble's long-range scheduling shipped in 1990 as SPIKE, a constraint-satisfaction system with heuristic repair (`1990aisi.conf....5J`), and JWST's scheduler still descends from it. The field then moved decisively to integer programming: Las Cumbres deployed the first production ILP for a telescope network in May 2014 (`2015arXiv150307170L`), ZTF's ILP scheduler has run nightly since 2018 (`2019PASP..131f8003B`, the anchor paper of the lineage), and 2021–2025 brought a wave of MILP systems for the Deep Space Network (`2021arXiv211111628C`), ALMA (`2016A&C....15...90S`), ZTF's gravitational-wave follow-up (`2022ApJ...935...87P`), geodetic VLBI (`2020JGeod..94...19C`), UVEX and the multi-mission M4OPT package (`2025arXiv250217560S`), and globally distributed relay arrays (`2025RAA....25a5008J`).

The formulations reward close reading because they are small, deployed, and reusable. Lampoudi's kernel is the cleanest base case: binary variables Y_ik for "reservation i starts at slot k," a precomputed occupancy map, priority-weighted acceptance as the objective, and a constraint matrix of only |I|+|T| rows, with multi-telescope allocation falling out of treating slots as (resource, timeslice) pairs. Bellm's ZTF scheduler shows what to do when direct sequencing is too big: cut the night into 15–25 blocks of thirty minutes, assign request-sets to blocks with an ILP maximizing volumetric survey speed, then solve an exact traveling-salesman tour over slew times within each block. Singer's UVEX work contributes the disjunctive-interval idiom, |t₀ − t₁| ≥ δ enforced through boolean order variables, and goes further by making per-field exposure time a continuous decision variable, so the depth-versus-breadth trade is made inside the solver rather than before it; the paper explicitly frames MILP as a "translation dictionary" from logical constraints to linear inequalities. Claudet's Δ-MILP for the DSN adds the operational trimmings a factory will also need, setup and teardown brackets around every track, minimum and maximum useful durations, multi-antenna arraying for oversized requests, and fairness terms balancing satisfied-time fractions across missions, validated against a real DSN week. Parazin's MUSHROOMS is the anytime exemplar: a modified max-weighted-coverage MILP over 951 simulated gravitational-wave skymaps with a 500-second solver cap, most instances proved optimal, the rest shipped as high-quality incumbents, with the authors noting a 100-second cap would have cost only 64 truncations.

Behind the applications sits a methodological literature the applications largely reinvented independently. The bead-graph-shaped problem, activities with precedence competing for renewable resources, is the resource-constrained project scheduling problem, and its recent results set expectations for anyone formulating one. Bold and Goerigk's compact reformulation of two-stage robust multi-mode RCPSP under Bertsimas–Sim budgeted uncertainty solves 93.1% of PSPLIB instances to optimality against 65.0% for an improved Benders decomposition, at speedups of 100–200× (`2022arXiv220306983B`); their stated lesson, that the easy-to-implement monolithic model dominates decomposition at this scale, contradicts the decompose-first instinct and is the single most budget-relevant result in the exact-methods literature. Kolter and Kolisch's systematic study of branch-and-price for RCPSP variants (`2025arXiv250104563K`) locates the boundary: column generation pays only when the variant gives the pricing problem real structure, and branching on resource demands yields strictly stronger bounds at the price of NP-hard pricing. When problems do get genuinely large and separable, Bragin and Tucker's surrogate level-based Lagrangian relaxation (`2022NatSR..1222417B`) coordinates per-subsystem MILPs through prices with hyperparameter-free step sizes, solving a 127-job stochastic job shop with scrap and rework in about an hour where branch-and-cut needed days. The reoptimization discipline, finally, has been formalized as a competition: MIPcc23 (`2023arXiv231114834B`) defines the regime of one fixed formulation with fifty perturbed data instances, scores solvers on time, gap, and feasibility failures, and its winning entry reused primal solutions and pseudo-costs across the series, the exact regime a factory re-solving on every queue event inhabits.

## 2. The reactive school won by re-deciding, not forecasting

The same operational problem produced a second, opposed literature, and the disagreement is the most valuable thing about it. Rubin/LSST rejected plan-based scheduling entirely. Naghib's framework (`2019AJ....157..151N`) formulates telescope scheduling as an MDP and then deliberately declines to solve it as one, replacing the value function with a memoryless policy, a weighted sum of handcrafted basis functions over the current state, with weights trained offline by evolutionary optimization against the operations simulator. The design carries its justification: evaluation is cheap enough for real time, no transition model is required, and because the policy depends only on current state, weather interruptions need no replanning at all. Ten years of survey operations across both camps produced a clean verdict that explicit stochastic modeling lost: ZTF handles a changing sky by re-solving a deterministic ILP, LSST by re-scoring a feature policy, and scenario-tree scheduling under night-loss uncertainty appears in this corpus only as a 2025 preprint (`2025arXiv250403666R`).

The corpus's stochastic-methods shelf still matters, but as targeted instruments rather than architecture. The bandit literature is the most immediately consumable: Slivkins's survey (`2019arXiv190407272S`) covers the standard machinery, Cayci et al. treat the budget-constrained case where every arm pull costs a random amount and the objective is reward before budget exhaustion (`2020arXiv200300365C`), and Yu, Xu, and Tong reduce deadline scheduling with stochastic service times to restless bandits with a per-job Whittle index, a single near-optimal priority scalar (`2016arXiv161000399Y`). Stochastic programming contributes structure theorems rather than systems: Basciftci's adaptive two-stage formulation, one revision point per resource between static two-stage and full multistage, comes with bounds quantifying how much of multistage's value the cheap structure captures (`2019arXiv190603513B`), and Siddig's rolling-horizon analysis makes lookahead depth a computed quantity, the shortest forecast horizon meeting a prescribed optimality gap (`2021arXiv210204874S`). Chance-constrained model predictive control formalizes probabilistic service-level constraints with constraint tightening (`2023arXiv230519262H`), heavy-traffic queueing covers deliberately-hot service systems including the recent regime where the service distributions themselves are only known to lie in broad uncertainty classes (`2022arXiv220405733A`), and Ruelens's two-settlement demand-response MDP (`2016ecc..conf..204R`) is the reserve-plus-spot capacity template.

One system paper stands apart for its evaluation methodology rather than its policy. Decima (`2018arXiv181001963M`) learns a DAG scheduler for data-processing clusters with a graph neural network and policy gradients, but its load-bearing contribution for any policy comparison is the variance-reduction discipline: training and evaluation fix the same job-arrival trace across compared policies, because with stochastic arrivals the noise otherwise swamps the signal. The trick has nothing to do with RL and everything to do with comparing schedulers honestly.

## 3. Graph optimization: mature theory, biology-tested tools, one sharp caution

The network-optimization shelf divides into breakthrough theory, workhorse algorithms with unexpected battle-testing, and one applied paper whose negative result matters more than most positive ones. The theory headline is Chen et al.'s almost-linear-time algorithm for both max flow and min-cost flow (`2022arXiv220300671C`), which abandons combinatorial augmentation for interior-point methods whose steps are approximate min-ratio cycle computations on a dynamically maintained graph; van den Brand et al. derandomized it the following year (`2023arXiv230916629V`). The practical consequence is that min-cut, the natural formalization of "sever a dependency structure at its weakest seam," is now cheap enough to run as a repeated per-commit query on graphs of monorepo scale.

The clustering workhorses have a longer paper trail. Louvain (`2008JSMTE..10..008B`) greedily maximizes modularity at O(n log n)-ish cost; Leiden (`2019NatSR...9.5233T`) exists because Louvain's communities can be arbitrarily badly connected, including outright disconnected, and adds a refinement phase that guarantees connectivity while running faster. Two corpus observations sharpen the choice. Forward-citation traversal from the Leiden paper lands overwhelmingly in single-cell genomics, because scanpy and Seurat ship Leiden as their default clustering, so the deployment lore for Leiden at scale lives in biology pipelines rather than network-science papers. And the software-engineering application is already published, if thinly: Raji et al. refactor packages via community detection (`2018arXiv181110171R`), with earlier network-structural studies of software by Šubelj and Serrano (`2011PhyA..390.2968S`, `2012arXiv1201.3771S`). Community detection for modularity exists; flow-based boundary placement does not, in this corpus or apparently anywhere.

Partitioning and DAG scheduling round out the shelf. The multilevel paradigm, coarsen, partition, refine, dates to Hendrickson and Leland (`1995supe.conf...24H`) and anchors the METIS family; spectral methods carry guarantees on mesh-like graphs (`1996sfcs.conf...12S`); heterogeneous DAG scheduling has its standard one-phase answer in HEFT-style upward-rank list scheduling, with critical-path length as the makespan lower bound. The applied caution comes from the Square Kilometre Array's DALiuGE dataflow system (`2018arXiv180507568W`), which partitions task-and-data DAGs at terabyte-per-second scale under a constraint that concurrently-running resource demand per partition stays under node capacity, merging greedily while a theorem keeps completion time monotonically non-increasing. Its authors state plainly why the obvious transfer fails: off-the-shelf graph partitioners give sub-optimal dataflow schedules because a static graph does not encode the temporal working set, which is both much smaller than the graph and unknown at partition time. For any system tempted to optimize a whole repository dependency graph at once, that is the most transferable sentence in the network literature.

## 4. Optimization theory: duality as pricing, landscapes as diagnosis, certificates as stopping rules

The theory shelf contributes vocabulary and guardrails more than algorithms. Duality's practical face is the shadow price, the multiplier on a binding constraint read as the marginal value of relaxing it, and the literature supplies its own warning label: Khabarov et al. show shadow-price-derived cost estimates are local marginal quantities whose finite extrapolations can be badly wrong, with a ratio of two shadow prices amounting to "an arbitrary number" (`2022arXiv221103591K`). The discipline that survives the caution, relax one constraint, re-solve, read fresh prices, is exactly how the multiplier is safe to use. On the constructive side, Cifuentes et al. give conditions under which dualizing the coupling constraints of a block-structured problem both closes the duality gap and decomposes the solve (`2024arXiv241112085C`), the formal version of "price the cross-cutting rules so the modules can be worked independently," and Gu, Ahmed, and Dey established zero-gap augmented-Lagrangian duality for mixed-integer QPs (`2019arXiv190700920G`).

The local-versus-global question has its richest treatment not in the optimization-methods literature but in chemical physics. Wales and Doye's basin-hopping (`1998cond.mat..3344W`) transforms an energy landscape so every configuration inherits the value of its local minimum, then hops between basins with Metropolis acceptance, and on that transformed landscape found Lennard-Jones global minima to N=110 including the notoriously non-icosahedral LJ38 and LJ75 cases. Doye's companion analysis (`2000cond.mat..7338D`) explains when such machinery is needed at all: difficulty is governed by landscape topography, single-funnel landscapes yield to greedy descent while multi-funnel landscapes with high barriers trap it, a framing that transfers to any search over configuration spaces, codebases included. Aerospace supplies the applied nonlinear-programming canon, direct collocation and SQP for trajectory optimization (`2000JCoAM.120...27B`, `1991JGCD...14..981E`), successive convexification (`2020JGCD...43.1399S`), and Açıkmeşe's lossless convexification (`2013ITCST..21.2104A`), which enlarges a nonconvex feasible set to a convex one and proves the relaxed optimum solves the original problem, global optimality without a nonconvex solve. Certificate machinery generalizes that move: Lasserre-hierarchy flat truncation gives a checkable condition that a relaxation has reached the true global optimum (`2011arXiv1106.2384N`), and Yang and Carlone's certifiably-optimal perception line shows the pattern operating at scale (`2021arXiv210903349Y`). Two further results earn their citations in any acceptance-rule design: Oztoprak, Byrd, and Nocedal's SQP for problems whose objective and constraint evaluations are noisy (`2021arXiv211004355O`), the formal home of gates fed by flaky signals, and Dunbar, Sinha, and Schaefer's demonstration that multiobjective integer programs require dual bound *sets*, with Lagrangian and convex-hull relaxations mutually incomparable (`2023arXiv230908801D`), the theory-side echo of the empirical case against scalarization made in section 6.

## 5. Modeling languages: the pattern is model/data separation, and LLMs just joined the loop

The algebraic-modeling-language literature is thinner in this corpus than its influence deserves, the AMPL and GAMS primary sources are absent entirely, but the design rationale survives in the JuMP paper (`2015arXiv150801982D`), whose stated motivations read like a software-architecture document: embed models in larger programmatic workflows, interact with a running solver through callbacks, avoid regenerating a model across sequences of related instances, and support user extension to new problem classes. MathOptInterface (`2020arXiv200203447L`) supplies the solver-agnostic intermediate representation, Pyomo the Python lineage (`2009orci.book....3H`), and the energy-systems community demonstrates the ecosystem payoff: PyPSA chose Pyomo explicitly for solver-agnosticism and because sibling frameworks share the substrate (`2017arXiv170709913B`), making a common modeling layer a coordination technology, not merely a convenience.

The corpus's production astronomy schedulers, interestingly, skip the AML layer entirely and hand-build Gurobi models from Python objects, a monoculture (Gurobi academic licenses everywhere, alternatives never benchmarked) that constitutes a reproducibility gap none of the papers address. Two practice threads matter beyond tool choice. Exact solvers certify negatives: in Handley's Traveling Telescope experiments the local-search heuristic silently reported infeasibility on 7 of 360 instances where the MILP proved feasible schedules existed (`2024AJ....167...33H`), which is the strongest single argument for exactness in any system where "no work available" and "scheduler failed" must be distinguishable. And infeasibility diagnostics via irreducible infeasible subsystems, the solver's ability to name the minimal conflicting constraint set, barely appear in applied use at all, a gap in practice rather than in capability.

The newest thread is the LLM-solver convergence, and it is moving fast. MCP-Solver (`2025arXiv250100539S`) connects an LLM to MiniZinc with item-indexed model editing, per-edit validation that rejects invalid edits with the parser error, strict separation of editing from solving, and model state held outside the LLM's context; Text2Zinc (`2025arXiv250310642S`) benchmarks natural-language-to-model translation and finds LLMs "not yet push-button" modelers, with compositional prompting through a knowledge-graph intermediate beating direct generation. Between them they sketch the division of labor this review's companion paper builds on: the model authors and revises the declarative model, the solver solves it, and the boundary is validated at every crossing.

## 6. Search-based software engineering is the one native literature, and its lessons are mostly warnings

SBSE is the corpus's only software-engineering-native optimization tradition, reachable through the arXiv cs.SE mirror, and its most durable findings are cautionary. Chen, Li, and Yao's survey of SBSE for self-adaptive systems (`2020arXiv200108236C`) documents that weighted-sum fitness aggregation dominates practice with essentially no published justification, and shows experimentally that weighted-GA solutions are dominated by Pareto-search solutions; scalarizing a multi-objective problem is the field's recorded unforced error. Schröder et al.'s industrial remodularization study at Adyen, 5.5 million lines (`2021arXiv210200701S`), documents the second failure mode: search operators learned to game the modularity metric monotonically by moving single classes into fresh modules, and metric improvement did not predict developer acceptance, leaving human review as the effective gate. Between them these two papers justify, empirically, the two design rules the companion paper leans on hardest: keep objectives vector-valued, and never let the optimization target double as the acceptance judge.

The constructive SBSE findings cluster around representation and evaluation economics. Three independent lines converged on encoding solutions as edit scripts rather than target states: Mkaouer's many-objective remodularization encodes variable-length operation sequences (`2020arXiv200506510W`), Adyen's system represents solutions as change sets because incremental fitness on diffs is what makes evaluation affordable, and ARJA's program-repair genome deliberately decouples location, operation, and ingredient subspaces (`2017arXiv171207804Y`). On evaluation cost, the field's reproducible embarrassment is that clever optimizers often fail to beat trivial baselines: Chen et al.'s SWAY, random oversampling plus recursive halving, matches evolutionary algorithms at orders of magnitude fewer evaluations and was proposed as the mandatory baseline (`2016arXiv160807617C`), while Nair, Yu, and Menzies's FLASH replaces population search with sequential model-based sampling, one decision tree per objective, and was the only optimizer to terminate on their hardest configuration models (`2017arXiv170505018N`); Bayesian methods extend the same logic to continuous spaces, with FABOLAS evaluating on data subsets and extrapolating (`2016arXiv160507079K`, `2012arXiv1206.2944S`). The LLM era has already been grafted onto this architecture: Gao et al. place LLM-generated proposals inside a search loop for code optimization (`2024arXiv240812159G`), the propose-with-a-model, select-deterministically pattern under its newest guise.

The metaheuristics classics survive in the corpus mainly through astronomy. Charbonneau's PIKAIA (`1995ApJS..101..309C`, 589 citations) chose an inefficient decimal encoding for debuggability and drives its mutation rate adaptively from population fitness contrast, a built-in premature-convergence alarm; gravitational-wave searches run multiple independent particle swarms to bound the probability of missing a global peak (`2010PhRvD..81f3002W`); Kirkpatrick's quantitative simulated-annealing studies anchor cooling-schedule design (`1984JSP....34..975K`). And a 2003 telescope-scheduling paper by Gómez de Castro and Yáñez (`2003A&A...403..357G`) quietly pre-empts SBSE's later methodology findings: their tuned genetic algorithm and tuned neighborhood search tied at about 0.66 telescope occupancy, leading them to conclude that the scheduling policy encoded in the fitness function, not the search algorithm, is the design surface that matters.

## 7. What the two literatures never told each other, and what neither told us

The cross-literature findings are the review's synthesis. The astronomy scheduling community and the SBSE community discovered the same lessons independently, policy-in-fitness dominance and algorithm-choice parity appear in the 2003 A&A paper years before SBSE formalized them, and the two bodies of work do not cite each other at all. The astronomy community also kept rebuilding the same slot-assignment ILP from scratch, LCO in 2015, ZTF in 2019, WFST and IPROS in 2024–25, with the first deliberately multi-mission open-source solver framework, M4OPT, arriving only in 2025; a field that shares a problem but not a kernel pays for it repeatedly, which is itself a design lesson for factory builders deciding whether the scheduler is a product or a per-team script.

The gaps are equally specific. The corpus holds no application of any of this machinery to software factories, CI systems, merge queues, or agent fleets. Flow-based module-boundary placement is unpublished. IIS-driven conflict diagnosis is nearly absent even where the solvers support it. Canonical OR and EMO primary sources, Deb's NSGA-II, Glover's tabu search, Dorigo's ant colony work, the AMPL and GAMS books, CVXPY's JMLR paper, are missing as first-class records and reachable only through reference lists, so any scholarly follow-up must route around ADS for those. Test-suite minimization proper is thin where prioritization is well covered. Each of these is either a claimable research contribution or a known hole to cite around, and the companion paper's proposals were shaped to land in the claimable set.

What would settle the transfer's value is the experiment neither literature has run: solver-informed dispatch against first-come-first-served and against a trivially tuned priority baseline, on identical replayed work-queue traces from a production agent fleet, with schedule stability and silent-infeasibility rates reported alongside throughput. The observatories ran their version of it over a decade of nights. The factory version is unclaimed.
